*Making pricing, approvals, and deal decisions faster and smarter.*

Anyone who has worked in sales operations or Deal Desk knows how complicated closing a deal can become.

A salesperson wants to offer a discount to win an important customer. Finance wants to protect margins. Legal needs to review contract terms. Leadership wants to make sure the deal makes business sense.

And somewhere in the middle, the Deal Desk team is trying to bring everyone together and get the deal approved before the customer loses interest.

This is where AI is starting to make a real difference.

AI is not going to replace Deal Desk teams. But it can certainly change how they work.

## The Real Challenge with Deal Desk Today

In many companies, Deal Desk processes still involve a lot of manual work.

Sales representatives submit discount requests, pricing exceptions, or special contract terms. Deal Desk analysts review these requests, look up pricing guidelines, compare previous deals, and coordinate approvals with different teams.

Sometimes, even a relatively simple deal requires multiple emails, spreadsheet calculations, and several rounds of clarification.

The problem is not always the approval process itself. Often, it is the amount of time spent collecting information before anyone can make a decision.

Imagine if AI could do much of that preparation.

## 1. Making Pricing Decisions Easier

Let's say a sales representative is negotiating a three-year software contract with a customer.

The customer is asking for a 25% discount.

Today, the salesperson might approach Deal Desk to understand whether that discount is acceptable. The Deal Desk team may need to review similar deals, check company pricing policies, understand profitability, and determine who needs to approve the request.

With AI, much of this information can be available almost immediately.

An AI assistant connected to CRM, CPQ, and relevant pricing data could answer questions like:

- What discounts have we offered to similar customers?
- How does this deal compare with other deals of similar size?
- Is the requested discount within our pricing guidelines?
- How would the discount affect the expected deal margin?
- Does this deal require additional approvals?

Instead of spending time gathering information, the Deal Desk analyst can focus on evaluating the deal and making a recommendation.

One important point: AI should not decide the final selling price on its own. It should provide useful information that helps people make better pricing decisions.

## 2. Reducing the Back-and-Forth in Approvals

Deal approvals can become frustrating, especially when multiple teams are involved.

A salesperson submits a request, only to discover that important information is missing. The request goes back to sales, gets updated, and is submitted again.

AI can help avoid some of this unnecessary back-and-forth.

Before an approval request is submitted, AI can review the deal and identify missing information.

For example, it might flag that:

- The proposed discount exceeds the standard limit.
- The payment terms are different from company policy.
- The business justification for the discount is missing.
- A required finance or legal review has not been included.

AI can also help prepare a short summary for approvers, explaining the deal value, requested exceptions, business reasons, and potential risks.

This saves approvers from having to go through long email threads just to understand what they are being asked to approve.

The actual approval decision can still remain with the right people.

## 3. Helping Sales Teams During Negotiations

This is another area where AI can add value.

Sales teams often enter negotiations without having enough information about what worked in similar deals.

For example, a customer may ask for an additional 10% discount just before signing the contract.

Instead of simply lowering the price, the salesperson could use an AI assistant to explore other options.

Perhaps the company could offer a longer contract term, adjust payment terms within policy, change the product mix, or offer additional services that provide value without reducing the overall deal margin as much.

AI could suggest these options based on company policies and previous deal patterns.

Of course, every customer and negotiation is different. AI recommendations need to be reviewed with that context in mind.

But having this information available during negotiations can help sales teams make more informed decisions.

## 4. Identifying Potential Deal Risks Early

Not every deal that looks attractive from a revenue perspective is necessarily a good deal for the business.

A large contract might include unusual payment terms, heavy discounts, expensive service commitments, or contractual conditions that create problems later.

Traditionally, identifying these risks requires people from different departments to carefully review the deal.

AI can help by examining information across the quote, opportunity, contract, and pricing records.

For example, it could highlight situations where a deal includes a large discount combined with unfavorable payment terms or where a contract contains terms that differ from the company's standard agreements.

This does not eliminate the need for finance or legal review.

Instead, it gives those teams a better starting point and helps them focus on areas that deserve attention.

## 5. Giving Deal Desk More Time for Important Work

Perhaps the biggest benefit of AI is not automation itself.

It is what Deal Desk teams can do with the time they save.

Think about how much time is currently spent looking for information, reviewing spreadsheets, preparing approval summaries, or following up on incomplete requests.

If AI can reduce that work, Deal Desk professionals can spend more time on activities that actually influence business outcomes.

They can work with sales leaders on deal strategy, identify pricing trends, improve discount policies, and understand why certain deals are profitable while others are not.

In other words, Deal Desk can move beyond processing requests and become a stronger partner in revenue decisions.

## But AI Is Only as Good as the Information Behind It

There is one challenge that companies should not ignore.

AI needs reliable information.

If pricing rules are outdated, CRM records are incomplete, or approval policies are unclear, introducing AI will not magically solve those problems.

In fact, it could create more confusion by making recommendations based on incorrect information.

Companies need to pay attention to their underlying systems, data quality, pricing policies, and approval processes.

AI also needs clear boundaries around what it can recommend, what it can do automatically, and what requires human approval.

A good starting point is to introduce AI into smaller activities such as preparing deal summaries, checking for missing information, or helping users find pricing policies.

Once those activities are working reliably, companies can explore more advanced use cases.

## Final Thoughts

I believe AI has a real opportunity to improve how Deal Desk teams operate.

But the biggest change may not come from automating every approval or allowing AI to negotiate deals independently.

It will come from making the right information available at the right time.

When sales representatives have better pricing guidance, approvers have better deal summaries, and Deal Desk analysts spend less time chasing information, the entire process becomes more efficient.

And that is where AI can make a meaningful impact.

**The future of Deal Desk is not about removing people from deal decisions. It is about giving them better tools to make those decisions.**
